Why Schools Should Teach Financial Literacy as a Core Subject Financial literacy is the ability to understand and manage money wisely. It includes skills such as budgeting, saving, investing, and avoiding unnecessary debt. These skills are important for everyday life, yet financial literacy is still not a core subject in many schools, including those in Indonesia. First, financial education helps young people make better financial decisions. Research from the Organization for Economic Co-operation and Development (OECD) shows that students who receive financial education are generally better at managing money and making responsible financial choices. According to data from the Otoritas Jasa Keuangan people still have limited knowledge of basic financial concepts. This suggests that many people still have limited knowledge of basic financial concepts. Second, schools can help students who may not receive financial guidance at home. Not all families have the same knowledge or experience in managing money. By teaching financial literacy in school, all students have the opportunity to learn essential financial skills regardless of their background. Third, learning about money at a young age helps students develop positive habits. Habits such as saving regularly, planning expenses, and thinking carefully before spending money can be useful throughout life. Some people believe that parents should be responsible for teaching these skills. However, not all parents have enough time or knowledge to do so. Therefore, schools can play an important role in preparing students for adult life.
