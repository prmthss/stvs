How to Conduct a Basic Soil pH Test at Home Materials needed: Soil sample from your garden or field Distilled water (available at pharmacies or supermarkets) pH test strips (range 4.0–8.0, available online or at agricultural supply stores) Two clean glass jars or cups A fine-mesh strainer or coffee filter A small measuring spoon Steps: 1. Collect the soil sample. Dig approximately 10 centimetres below the surface and take a sample of about 2 tablespoons of soil. Avoid topsoil that may contain fertilizer residue, compost, or surface debris. Use a clean, dry tool to prevent contamination of the sample. 2. Prepare the mixture. Place the soil into one of the clean jars. Add 60 millilitres (approximately 4 tablespoons) of distilled water. Tap water must not be used, as it contains chlorine and minerals that can significantly distort pH readings. 3. Mix thoroughly and allow to settle. Stir the mixture vigorously for at least one full minute. Then, leave it undisturbed for a minimum of 15 minutes to allow the solid particles to settle to the bottom. 4. Filter the liquid. Carefully pour the settled liquid through the fine-mesh strainer or coffee filter into the second clean jar. The resulting liquid should be relatively clear. If the liquid remains murky, filter it a second time. 2–5 seconds). Remove the strip and lay it flat on a clean, dry surface. Do not shake or wipe the strip. 6. Read and record the result. After waiting the indicated time (usually 30–60 seconds), compare the color of the strip against the color chart provided in the packaging. Record the pH value. A reading of 6.0–7.0 generally indicates suitable soil for most garden vegetables. Values below 5.5 suggest the soil is acidic and may benefit from lime treatment, while values above 7.5 indicate alkalinity and may require sulphur application. Important Note: This home test provides an estimate only. For agricultural or scientific decisions, a laboratory soil analysis is strongly recommended.
