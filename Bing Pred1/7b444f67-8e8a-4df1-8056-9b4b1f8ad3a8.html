How to Play Padel Padel is a fast-paced, highly engaging racket sport that combines elements of tennis and squash. Typically played in doubles on an enclosed glass court, it is easy to learn but offers plenty of room for tactical growth. If you are ready to hit the court with your friends, follow this straightforward guide to playing your first match. Equipment and Setup Before stepping onto the court, ensure you have the correct gear. Unlike tennis, padel uses a solid, stringless racket made of composite materials with perforated holes. The balls look similar to tennis balls but have slightly less pressure. Secure the safety lanyard of the racket around your wrist. This is a mandatory safety rule to prevent the racket from flying out of your hand during intense rallies. Step-by-Step Instructions 1. The Serve: Every point begins with an underhand serve. Stand behind the service line and bounce the ball on the service box. 2. The Glass Wall Rule: before hitting any surrounding glass walls or wire fences. If it hits the wall directly without bouncing first, it is out. 3. The Rally: Once the ball bounces, your opponents can hit it back or let it rebound off the glass walls before striking it. You can also use your own glass walls to smash the ball back over the net. 4. Scoring: Padel utilizes the traditional tennis scoring system: 15, 30, 40, and game. Winning six games wins a set, and matches are usually decided by the best of three sets. Remember: Communication with your partner is the ultimate key to dominating the court!
