The Journey of Alfred Nobel: A Life of Innovation Alfred Nobel was born in Stockholm, Sweden, on October 21, 1833. His father, Immanuel Nobel, was an engineer and inventor who built infrastructures and experimented with rock-blasting techniques. His mother, Andriette Ahlsell, came from a wealthy family. During his youth, the family relocated to St. Petersburg, Russia, where his father ran a successful mechanical workshop. In Russia, Alfred received a high-quality education from private tutors, quickly mastering natural sciences and five foreign languages. It was there that he became deeply fascinated by nitroglycerine, a highly volatile explosive liquid. Upon returning to Sweden in 1863, Alfred focused his energy on finding a method to control the detonation and safe handling of this substance. Through extensive experimentation, Alfred discovered that mixing liquid nitroglycerine with a fine sand called kieselguhr turned it into a manageable paste. In 1867, he patented this groundbreaking material under the name “dynamite.” To ignite it safely, he also invented the blasting cap. These tools revolutionized construction, mining, and industrial drilling worldwide. As a skilled entrepreneur, Alfred established around 90 factories and laboratories across more than 20 countries. He traveled constantly, holding a total of 355 patents by the end of his life. On December 10, 1896, Alfred Nobel passed away in San Remo, Italy. In his final will and testament, he dedicated his vast fortune to establishing the prestigious Nobel Prizes, honoring achievements that brought the greatest benefit to mankind.
